## Findings

### F1 · High · Permanent exclusions exhaust offline review

For whole $w$, the unordered nonnegative two-part splits number $\lfloor w/2\rfloor+1$. Across wholes 0–10 there are 36. Make-ten has six, from 0+10 through 5+5, and five if both parts must be positive. Treating known and missing parts as ordered produces 11 prompts at most, with substantial information shared between reversals. U10's 6+4 and U11's 7+3 consume two relationships if scored or disclosed, leaving four unordered relationships including zero. Even four initial successes plus three two-item reviews require ten distinct keys. Allowing one initial error consumes eleven. Each recurring review consumes two more. Worked examples, diagnostics with revealing feedback, cross-task disclosure, and recovery checks can only shrink the remaining stock. The bound gets tighter when essential facets or accessible comparable items are required. More seeds, wording or layouts cannot extend the lifetime of the policy. Long-term review needs deliberate re-use of learned facts. Evidence of delayed retrieval of a familiar relationship and evidence of transfer to a different relationship support different claims. [CUR-003 U10 and U11](../first-unit.md#u11-assessment-and-later-recheck)

### F2 · High · Initial evidence needs a defined check group and a stopping rule

Four correct first responses across two dates is a plausible conservative beta signal if the successes cover essential facets and one confirmed error can coexist with otherwise sufficient evidence. The draft's “at most the last five” permits 4/4 whereas “4-of-5” implies five completed opportunities. A moving window can drop the only item for a facet or let successive optional attempts wash out repeated difficulty. Define whether the date requirement applies to the successes themselves. Four successes in one sitting plus an error on a second date would meet a date rule on opportunities while demonstrating no successful retrieval on the second date. Preselect coverage, including any required contrast such as zero, without requiring a separate five-item group for every superficial variant. A broad label requires evidence for the broad claim. Keep partial coverage visible and check whether the cumulative burden across U11's separate bounded skills fits the unit's 10–15 minute sessions. Pausing, opting for instruction, unavailable coverage and access uncertainty need a pending outcome with no forced repeats or scored error. [CUR-003 pacing and U11](../first-unit.md)

### F3 · High · Retention observations are too sparse for an automatic regression rule

A two-item check is a manageable sample. Immediate closure on one confirmed error makes the second complementary observation unavailable and treats an isolated lapse as equivalent to consistent difficulty. A first 2-day pass supports a dated observation of early retention. It leaves longer-delay performance open. The proposed 2/7/21 are successive gaps after the preceding achievement or pass, so on-time reviews fall 2, 9 and 30 days after initial mastery. Recovery after a single next-day pass would restore the same label and restart at seven days without specifying the effect on the previously evidenced interval. The contract also needs explicit handling for late review, partial completion and feedback that cues the next item. Research supports delayed retrieval and varying spacing with retention horizon. The cited experiments do not establish these cutoffs, intervals or sample sizes for P1 number bonds. [Roediger and Karpicke, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/) · [Cepeda et al., 2008](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0kp5q19x)

### F4 · High · Model evidence requires a trustworthy learner action

For U07, a sufficient model links the stated whole to two disjoint, exhaustive groups, binds the known and missing roles and agrees with the confirmed answer. An empty group must be explicit when zero is being demonstrated. Accept learner-built counters or learner-selected and marked groups with the same semantics through drag, tap or VoiceOver. Require observable learner choices about the grouping and whole. An app-populated bond, automatic complement fill or accessibility wording that announces the missing quantity changes the task. The fixture's observed 8/3/5 tuple is a useful format example, but it cannot establish disjointness, authorship or role assignment by itself. Verify against a pinned semantic state or equivalent structured marking. Save free drawing and optionally let the learner mark its groups using accessible controls. If those marks do not establish the required relation, model evidence stays pending or can receive a separately identified rubric-based review under an approved observation route. A numerical 5 alone can earn correct numerical evidence. An intentionally submitted, complete contradictory model yields model error. Incomplete marking, rejected moves and ambiguous work require clarification. Define whether recombination itself is being assessed. If so, a sufficient final partition alone cannot prove recombination and the rubric needs an observed action or equivalent explanation.

Recognition correction before confirmation, keypad entry and neutral prompt replay preserve the mathematical demand. Uncertain transcription creates no scored attempt. Keep printed-word recognition, word production and numeral formation measures separate from quantity and bond evidence. A reader pronouncing the target printed word supplies part of the literacy task. Offer a separate mathematics check when the route changes that literacy demand. ## Proposed owner decisions

These are beta policy options for approval and trial.

1. **Permit spaced reuse offline.** Prefer an unused comparable bond when sampling transfer. For retention, rotate eligible familiar bonds, prioritize the least recently prompted, and sample complementary facets without using the same relationship twice in a check group. Consider a conservative beta starting rule that a reused relationship must be on a different learning date and at least 48 elapsed hours after the last applicable interaction. Start the gap at the latest applicable presentation or feedback, including practice with the bond, rehearsal, task-specific help and mapped solution disclosure. Recheck at offer and submit, and keep the embargo through all channels and compatible versions. Retain distinct-key coverage within the initial group and permanently preserve historical receipts. A repeated bond after the gap can support delayed retrieval under the approved rubric. A recent disclosure of a value also blocks immediate same-value substitution when it invites echoing. Study genuinely different relationships where the target allows them and identify transfer observations explicitly. For example, when four of the six make-ten bonds have been checked, a later review can rotate two rested bonds under the proposed rule. If only one bond meets the gap and coverage rules, allow low-pressure practice or pause and defer the check until the full eligible sample is available. Avoid automatically assigning the same embargoed bond as practice whenever the review opens, since that can prevent the gap from ever elapsing. Local installed templates, history and a bounded scan suffice. For a more conservative beta, keep first-time-only credit for initial transfer samples and use recurring familiar-bond retrieval as its own review evidence.
2. **Choose an initial evidence floor and cap.** Prefer four complete successes in up to five prespecified scored opportunities, with successful evidence on at least two learning dates and required facet coverage. End at four if all checks are met. Save the planned slots and committed results across sittings. If the group finishes short or coverage is missing, surface the particular need, offer instruction and try a newly planned group later. An alternative for a narrow target is three complementary complete successes over two dates with a later corroborating sample before a broader label. Both are hypotheses. Test the actual item mix and burden before choosing, and avoid subdividing targets just to make thresholds easier to meet.
3. **Use short, staged retention checks.** Trial successive gaps of 2, 7 and 21 days, followed by a review cadence the owner approves. Start each interval from actual qualifying completion. A late visit opens one due check, without stacking missed checks or rewriting history. Prefer two complete first responses with complementary coverage. For 1/2, preserve prior achievement and show a follow-up due for the missed facet on a later eligible day. One successful comparable follow-up can corroborate the mixed sample under the approved rule. Repeated difficulty or two confirmed errors can prompt a refresh and a new delayed check. Alternatively retain 2/2 for passage, with mixed evidence treated as inconclusive pending a later two-item sample. Keep the evidenced interval and date visible, including when the first pass is only the short-delay stage. Define an optional early refresh after practice and a separate advancement rule for the longer interval. Define the review window and how long paused samples remain comparable. A late or incomplete sample should carry a due/pending status without changing the last earned achievement. Use an injected local clock and pinned date semantics, and test near-midnight sessions, travel, clock rollback and forward jumps. Under clock uncertainty preserve the last trustworthy record and defer spacing credit. The offline app can verify recorded local intervals under its clock rules.
4. **Approve evidence and routing semantics.** Confirm which structured or rubric-observed models can earn credit, which supports preserve each construct, whether recombination is separately required, the diagnostic recommendation rule and parent override provenance. Preserve a guided route and accessible modelling even when a learner elects to move on. Confirm that same-occurrence neutral retry is recorded separately and that the next independent opportunity always meets the reviewed demand and exposure rules.
