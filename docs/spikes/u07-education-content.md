# U07 · Split a whole

**Preparatory Education draft · en-SG · for review.** EDU-001 remains gated. The dialogue, timing and interactions below are proposed for a future playable slice.

The learner splits a counted whole into two groups, connects the groups to a number bond and shows that the parts still make the same whole. Digital counters serve as concrete manipulatives, the bond diagram or a drawing shows the groups pictorially, and numeral labels express the quantities abstractly. The math shorthand for reviewers is **6 = 2 + 4**, then **7 = 1 + 6**, then **8 = 3 + 5**. Children see a bond diagram with a whole and two parts. Formal operation notation comes later in the unit.

U07 maps to P1-L016 and P1-ADD-01-C01 under P1-ADD-01, printed p. 31, item 2.1. Counting and matching numerals draw on P1-NUM-01 and the bounded numeral–quantity work mapped under P1-NUM-02. The split and recombine sequence is the accepted unit’s instructional design. ## Playback and pacing

Every quotation is exact Juni dialogue and its verbatim caption. Product labels and actions outside quotations are screen or production directions. Use the same wording as step text in manual mode, with the relevant picture and state. The Observe times are draft offsets from clip start, in seconds. For other activities, speak at the listed trigger and show each caption from speech onset through the listed quiet beat or until the next learner action. Pause and replay are available. All quiet beats are pacing suggestions, with unlimited thinking time and no timed submissions. Plan about two minutes for readiness, three for Observe including replay and reflection, four for Try and three for Check. ### Readiness

In a separate workspace show six loose counters and numeral choices. Juni says “Touch each counter once as you count. Tap the number that tells how many.” Leave at least five quiet seconds and wait. Use fresh ungrouped sets for seven and eight as needed, using the same prompt. When counting needs support, say “Touch one counter for each number you say.” Give separate counting practice and recheck with a fresh arrangement. Keep the readiness workspace free of split diagrams or part labels. If readiness still needs support, the recommended path can return to practice and defer the scheduled Check. Readiness is advisory and creates no mastery prerequisite.

### Observe · Split six and put it back together

Start with six individually distinguishable counters in one tray and an empty bowl. Keep the learner controls for moving counters inactive during the demonstration. The count cues indicate each counter once. Use a thin outline for the indicated counter or group, with the hand positioned clear of counters and labels.

| Time | Juni dialogue and caption | Counter and diagram state |
|---|---|---|
| 00–05 | “Let’s count the counters in this tray.” | C1–C6 in tray. Bowl empty. Part roles unassigned. |
| 05–12 | “One, two, three, four, five, six.” | Indicate C1–C6 in order at 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10. Hold through 12. |
| 12–19 | “Six is the whole. That means all the counters. Watch me split them into two groups.” | Show **Whole 6** and link it to the full set. Hold for a beat before the first move. |
| 19–22 | “One into the bowl.” | Transfer C1 at 20. Tray 5, bowl 1. |
| 22–26 | “One more into the bowl. Two in the bowl.” | Transfer C2 at 23. Tray 4, bowl 2. |
| 26–32 | “The tray is our other group. How many are there?” | Assign tray the second part role at 26. Hold both groups in view and leave about two quiet seconds. |
| 32–38 | “One, two, three, four. Four in the tray.” | Indicate C3–C6 at 32, 33, 34, 35. |
| 38–49 | “Two in the bowl. Four in the tray. Two and four make six. This picture is a number bond.” | Show the linked diagram at 38. Indicate **Part 2** with the bowl at 38, **Part 4** with the tray at 40, and **Whole 6** with both at 42. Hold the complete diagram. |
| 49–60 | “Let’s put the two back. One, two, three, four, five, six. We still have six!” | At 51 return C1 then C2 at the same boundary and clear the active partition. Keep the diagram labelled **Our split** as a completed example. Indicate C1–C6 in the recombined tray at 52, 53, 54, 55, 56, 57. |
| 60–64 | “Ready to make your own split? Tap Try.” | Hold the six recombined counters and the completed example. Expose **Try** at clip end. |

Manual **Next step** follows the row start boundaries in order, applying the intervening moves and showing the same count and role information. Static count steps show the ordered counter indications and their count words, with no moving hand. The move rows show the resulting positions with C1 or C2 indicated. The final step shows the recombined tray and enables **Try**. Switching from playback keeps the current position and the next step continues from it. **Replay** starts from the six-counter baseline. Reopening Observe from Try preserves the learner’s counters, answer and working, and returns to that workspace.

### Try · Make a new split with seven

Tap **Try** to open a fresh workspace with seven loose counters in the tray, an empty bowl and bond labels **Whole 7**, **Part 1**, **Other part: blank**. These labels describe the task. Link **Part 1** to the bowl after the learner moves one. Leave the other part numeral blank until the learner enters it.

| Trigger | Juni dialogue and caption | Action and quiet beat |
|---|---|---|
| On entry | “Here are seven counters. Move one from the tray to the bowl.” | The learner drags one counter or selects a counter and then the bowl. Wait for the learner. |
| One in bowl | “The bowl has one. The counters in the tray are the other part. How many are in the tray?” | Link the bowl to **Part 1** and the tray to the blank part. Leave at least five quiet seconds to count. |
| Bond entry | “Put that number in the empty part of the number bond.” | Let the learner enter a numeral and confirm it. Wait for submission. |
| Correct numeral and split confirmed | “You made two groups. One and six make seven. Put the bowl counter back in the tray. How many are there now?” | The learner returns the one counter, restoring all seven to the tray. Clear the active partition and retain the completed diagram. Leave at least five quiet seconds to recount or reason. |
| After the learner verifies the recombined total | “Seven again! You split the whole and put it back together.” | Hold the finished workspace and offer a stopping point before Check. |

In manual mode, show each prompt when its trigger is met. Use **Next step** to reveal the bond entry prompt after the counting prompt, and let the learner return to the counters from entry. For each move, tap the counter and tap the destination, or use the same selection and destination actions with VoiceOver. The learner makes the moves and enters the answer in every mode.

If a response needs another look, start with “Try once more. How many are in the other part?” Pause and let the learner revise. Use the following help only when the work supports the observation.

| Observed work | Juni dialogue and caption | What to show |
|---|---|---|
| The learner enters the whole and points to both locations, or enters the known part and points to the bowl | “The bowl holds one part. Look at the tray for the other part.” | Outline the tray and link it to the blank. |
| The learner moves more than one counter to the bowl | “We need one in the bowl. Move counters back until just one is there.” | Keep both locations visible and let the learner correct the split. |
| The learner skips or counts a counter twice | “Touch each tray counter once. Say one number for each touch. The last number tells how many.” | Keep the tray still. Let the learner count. |
| The response is ambiguous and the learner asks for help | “Show me the two groups you made.” | Keep the workspace editable. Use the work shown to choose the next prompt. |
| More teaching is needed after guided help | “Let’s watch how one whole can make two parts.” | Offer the six-counter demonstration and then return to the learner’s work. |

### Check · Show your own split

Start a fresh workspace with **Whole 8**, **Known part 3**, and **Other part: blank**. The counter option starts with eight unpartitioned counters and an empty destination. The drawing option starts with blank working space. Neither option singles out a group or pre-fills the unknown.

| Trigger | Juni dialogue and caption | Action and quiet beat |
|---|---|---|
| On entry or prompt replay | “The whole is eight. One part is three. What is the other part? Show how your two parts make eight. You can use counters or draw.” | Leave at least eight quiet seconds and wait as long as needed. Offer both tools without selecting one for the learner. |
| Learner chooses to enter an answer | “Put your answer in the empty part.” | Offer the standard keypad or supported numeral handwriting with confirmation. Feedback waits for a confirmed submission. |
| Unclear handwriting candidate | “Is this the number you meant? You can change it or use the keypad.” | Show only the candidate transcribed from the learner’s entry. Let the learner confirm, edit or switch methods. |
| Learner has entered a numeral only and wants to add working | “Show how your two parts make eight.” | Reuse the original modelling request. Preserve the draft and keep the tools available. |
| Confirmed response and matching learner model committed | “You showed both parts and how they make eight. Well done!” | Keep the learner’s own model visible. |
| Confirmed response committed, modelling evidence pending | “You can show your thinking with counters or a drawing.” | Preserve the submitted numeral and record modelling separately under the applicable policy. |
| Confirmed error or inconsistent response and model | “Take another look. The whole is eight. One part is three. What is the other part? Show how your two parts make eight.” | Re-present the original givens and leave the learner’s work visible. Apply the approved retry policy. |

In manual or muted mode, use the same prompt, tool labels, input and feedback in the same order. VoiceOver announces the givens, blank and control labels and makes the same counter selection and destination actions available. Accessible marking can show the learner’s two groups and the whole. Labels and live descriptions report the learner’s actual actions and entries. They provide no automatic group selection or missing-part total. Pause and resume preserve working, answer draft and assistance state.

**Reviewer rubric.** Expect confirmed **5** in the missing-part slot and a learner-produced model showing disjoint groups of three and five that together account for all eight. The learner can group counters, draw and distinguish the groups, or mark the parts and whole accessibly. Recombining or identifying both groups together as eight can demonstrate the relationship. A spoken explanation can accompany the model. A bare 5 leaves modelling pending. Check for whole-as-part (8), known-part repetition (3), overlapping groups, and a miscount. Use the neutral feedback above before offering any task-specific help. A numeral alone cannot diagnose a misconception, and an uncertain transcription is an input issue.

If the learner needs mathematical help, offer “We can practise a different split together.” Use the guided seven-counter activity as separate practice. Record applicable exposure before delivering any task-specific scaffold or disclosure through speech, text or visuals. Preserve evidence already committed. After help, offer an eligible fresh item of comparable demand under the applicable policy, or leave independent evidence pending if none is available. Prompt replay, manual progression and equivalent input carry the original information only. Media and timer events create zero attempts.
